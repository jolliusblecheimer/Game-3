# Tenka II — Age of Steam (蒸気の時代)

*A plan for a Japanese steampunk continuation of Tenka. Nothing here is built yet. This is where the ideas are collected before work starts.*

---

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

You united the realm. Peace lasts a generation, then black ships appear on the horizon, and with them coal, steam and brass. Tenka II picks up where the Shogunate era ends. Your castle town becomes a smoking, clanking city of *karakuri* (からくり, clockwork automata), steam mills and rail yards. Airships hang over the rice terraces, and rival houses race to master the new machines. It keeps what makes Tenka good (a living village, sieges you command yourself, eras to climb) and adds industry, railways and a sky to fight in.

**Working title options:** *Tenka II: Age of Steam* · *Tenka: Karakuri* · *Kikai no Tenka* (機械の天下, "the realm of machines").

---

## 2. Setting and tone

- **Alternate history, late Edo into Meiji (about 1850–1900),** where steam arrives early and fuses with Japanese craft instead of replacing it.
- **Look:** lacquered wood and brass. Copper boilers with vermilion trim. Paper lanterns next to gas lamps. Tiled roofs with smokestacks. Rust and moss. Clockwork cranes (*tsuru*) that bow like the birds. Shinto rope around a boiler to calm its spirit.
- **Tone:** warm and a bit whimsical, not grim. Machines have personalities. A karakuri tea-serving doll that becomes a soldier is the heart of the style.
- **Colour palette:** keep Tenka's cream, ink and vermilion, then add brass (#b58a3c), oxidised copper (#5f8f7a), soot (#2b2a28) and steam white.

---

## 3. How it connects to Tenka

- **Continue a save:** after victory in Tenka ("Tenka — the land is yours!"), a new button appears: *"A new age dawns…"*.
  - It carries over your village layout, your veterans (now old masters who train others), your Great Buildings (as heritage sites with a small bonus) and your clan colours.
  - The Keep becomes the **Castle Works** (城工廠).
- **Or start fresh** in a new province with a modest starting kit.
- **Same engine:** three.js, the procedural Mesher models, the village grid, raids, battles and the map all carry over and get extended, not rewritten.

## 4. Eras (continuing the numbering from Tenka)

| # | Era | Kanji | What opens |
|---|-----|-------|------------|
| 7 | Black Ships | 黒船 | Coal, the first steam engine, a harbour, trade with foreigners |
| 8 | Steam and Iron | 蒸気 | Foundries, rail yard, rifle ashigaru, steam rams |
| 9 | Karakuri | 絡繰 | Clockwork workers and soldiers, the automaton workshop |
| 10 | Rails of the Realm | 鉄道 | Railways on the country map, armoured trains, telegraph |
| 11 | Sky Lanterns | 空灯 | Airships, sky docks, raids from the air, observation balloons |
| 12 | The Brass Shogunate | 真鍮幕府 | Final wonders; the realm becomes an industrial empire (ending) |

Research keeps the **Wisdom** system, but engineers produce a second currency, **Know-how** (技). Technologies need a mix: tradition needs Wisdom, machines need Know-how.

---

## 5. New resources

| Resource | Where it comes from | What it's for |
|----------|---------------------|---------------|
| Coal (石炭) | Coal pits in the hills | Fuel for every machine; stockpile it for winter |
| Copper (銅) | Copper mines | Boilers, pipes, karakuri joints |
| Brass (真鍮) | Smelter: copper + zinc/ore | Automata, instruments, high-tier upgrades |
| Steam pressure | Boiler houses (not stored) | Powers nearby buildings, like electricity |
| Silk (絹) | Silk farms and mills | Export, balloon envelopes, trade with the black ships |

**Steam network:** boiler houses power buildings within a radius, or through **pipes** laid like roads. It's a second network on top of the road rule. Buildings can be *unpowered* (slow, done by hand) or *powered* (fast, automatic).

---

## 6. New buildings (village)

- **Boiler House** (汽罐所): burns coal and makes steam pressure for its area. It can explode if overloaded, a new kind of event.
- **Foundry** (鋳造所): iron and copper become parts. The upgraded blacksmith.
- **Steam Rice Mill**: fields feed it; much more food per farmer.
- **Karakuri Workshop** (絡繰工房): builds clockwork workers (never tired, need oil and repair) and later clockwork soldiers.
- **Rail Yard / Station**: connects the village to the rail network on the map.
- **Telegraph Office**: early raid warnings from far away; see enemy armies moving on the map.
- **Sky Dock** (空港): builds and launches airships.
- **Gas Lamps**: decoration that also lights the night (fewer sneak attacks).
- **Tea House of Automata**: a harmony building where karakuri dolls serve tea; big mood boost.
- **Merchant Consulate**: trade with foreign ships in the harbour for rare goods and blueprints.

**New wonders (Great Buildings):**
- **The Great Clock Tower of Edo:** Know-how production.
- **Iron Pagoda:** a five-storey pagoda with gears turning inside; mood and research.
- **The Dragon Engine:** a colossal locomotive shrine; rail speed and coal.
- **Floating Castle** (浮城): a castle keep lifted by balloons; air defence and prestige.
- **Karakuri Kannon:** a thousand-armed automaton; repairs machines and heals.

---

## 7. Military

**New units:**
- **Teppō rifle ashigaru:** long range, slow reload, strong behind walls.
- **Steam ram / siege crawler:** armoured, self-propelled, puffs smoke.
- **Karakuri samurai:** clockwork warriors. Fearless and tough, but they wind down and must be re-wound by an engineer.
- **Kite-riders and gliders:** land behind walls; the successors to ninja.
- **Airship** (飛行船): bombs structures, spots hidden units, and can be shot down by fire arrows or rifles.
- **Armoured train:** moves only on rails, but it's a moving fortress.
- **Engineers:** repair machines and walls mid-battle, and build barricades.

**Battles:**
- **Height matters:** airships fly over walls, and anti-air (fire-arrow towers, rocket batteries) becomes a new defence layer.
- **Smoke:** steam engines leave smoke that blocks sight. It's the steampunk version of fog, and you can use it for stealth.
- **Machines break:** a hit boiler can explode and hurt everything nearby.
- **Rails** on some battlefields: the defenders' train arrives with reinforcements unless you blow the track.

**Village raids:**
- **Sky pirates** (空賊) attack from the air: they drop raiders onto rooftops and grab loot from storehouses.
- **Rival houses** send steam crawlers that shrug off arrows, so you need rifles or pitfall traps.
- **Sabotage:** a spy sneaks in and cuts your steam pipes; find him before the machines stop.

---

## 8. The country map

- **Railways replace roads:** lay track between places you hold. Trains move armies and goods fast, but track can be raided and cut.
- **Airship routes:** straight lines over mountains, and they reveal fog as they fly.
- **The harbour and the black ships:** foreign traders visit on a schedule, and you choose which foreign "house" to favour (diplomacy with outsiders).
- **Industrial places:** coal fields, copper hills and silk towns to take and hold.
- **Weather on the map:** typhoon season grounds airships; snow closes mountain passes.

---

## 9. Life in the village

- **Smog and mood:** factories lower harmony nearby. Parks, shrines and tea houses near them fix it, so layout matters even more.
- **Workers' guilds:** engineers, stokers and dollmakers; new jobs with their own needs.
- **Festivals:** the Festival of Lanterns and Steam; night parades of glowing karakuri floats.
- **Speech bubbles** grow new personalities: grumbling stokers, proud engineers, children chasing a clockwork dog.

---

## 10. Sound and music

- **Music:** the koto and shakuhachi meet a music-box / calliope melody. Mix-style rotation like Tenka's, with a new "Clockwork" style.
- **Sound effects:** hiss of steam, clanking gears, train whistles, the heavy thump of a boiler, ticking automata, airship propellers.

## 11. Technical plan (in the existing code)

1. **Eras 7–12** extend `ERAS` / `TECHS`. Add Know-how next to Wisdom (`state.knowhow`).
2. **A second network** (steam pipes) using the same flood fill as `computeLinks()`; buildings get `powered` next to `linked`.
3. **Resources:** add coal, copper, brass and silk to `RES` (`extra: true`, so they only show once you have them).
4. **Units:** new `UNITS` entries.
   - Flying units get a `fly` flag and a height, and ignore walls in pathing (straight lines).
   - Rifles are `ranged` with a long cooldown.
   - Machines get `wind` (clockwork running time).
5. **Models:** a brass/copper palette in `buildings.js` and `people.js`.
   - Helpers for smokestacks with the Smoke class from `weather.js`.
   - Turning gears, animated per frame like the flags.
6. **Rails on the map:** reuse `CountryMap.buildRoads()` with a sleeper-and-rail mesh; `marchRoute()` gets a train speed.
7. **Airships:** a new mission kind on the map; in battles, a flying unit with a shadow.
8. **Saves:** `tenka.save.v2` loads an old Tenka save that has won and converts it (village kept, era 7 starts).
9. **Keep Tenka playable:** the classic `v1/` stays, and Tenka (v2) stays. The sequel could live in `steam/`, or be the next era after victory.

---

## 12. Milestones (suggested order)

1. **Black Ships:** coal and the boiler house, the steam network, harbour trade. *(Village only.)*
2. **Rifles and steam rams:** new units in battles and raids, smoke and sight.
3. **Karakuri:** clockwork workers and soldiers, winding, the workshop.
4. **Railways:** track on the map, trains, cutting rails.
5. **The sky:** airships in battles, sky-pirate raids, air defence.
6. **Wonders and ending:** the Brass Shogunate, the final epilogue.

---

## 13. Open questions

- Is it a separate game (new start, own save) or does it continue after Tenka's victory? *(Leaning: both. Continue after victory, and offer a fresh start.)*
- How much should machines replace people? Keeping villagers central probably keeps it warm.
- Real history (Perry's black ships, the Meiji era) or fully invented houses? *(Leaning: invented houses, historical flavour.)*
- Difficulty curve: the sky war must not make walls useless. Walls stay the main defence, and air defence is a second layer.
